SNAFU

1 The signal is digging in, 
2 digging trenches until disjunction inherits 
3 a troglodyte world1: 
4 assimilation, successful metaphor, 
5 and repetitive repetition
6 breed forgetfulness, 
7 so much so, digging out 
8 makes an uneasy task of up
9 and meeting out the square.
10
11 There, cars circle, 
12 circle the suicide circle; 
13 standing at the intersection, 
14 actuality starts digging in.
15 World turns wholly upside down,
16 every situation, situation normal all fucked up, 
17 and separation sinks in beyond all recognition.
18
19 Pray God holds the women and children.
20 Twenty-five thousand miles halve the Earth, 
21 dug in, literally disjunct,
22 with the lucky ones, 
23 not asleep, up and out 
24 among the dead,
25 where trees are torn from their beds
26 and sheets are strewn about 
27 over lying men’s heads:
28
29             Mud knee deep in some places, 
30             others deeper than one dared to venture, 
31             crawling about in the muck,
32             washed away by the runoff of the leaden rain.
33             Only way to bear the monstrous horror of it all 
34             was to look upward away the earthly reality . . . 
35             existing from moment to moment2, 
36
37 from half-sleep to waking-dream
38 of physical sanctimony
39 and gas-lit automaticrazy. 

11.26.10

1 Fussel, Paul. The Great War and Modern Memory. Oxford University Press, 1977.

2 Sledge, E. B. With the Old Breed: at Peleliu and Okinawa. Presidio Press, 2010.
