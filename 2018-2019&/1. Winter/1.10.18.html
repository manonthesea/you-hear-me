I write poems, written for my loves --

1 to be read in new-year winter, southern spring, hot summer, and quick fall
2 and to be read by those who humor me to speak with synecdoche
3 because they know the burning flame-red, soft hair trying my sanity.
4
5 I am a neighborhood poet, who lives and pains 
6 the same insufferable cul-de-sac experience 
7 as the housewife in her brick ranch down the block 
8 clinging to her presupposed, dull belief in a paradox.
9
10 Take these poems; let the word liberate us after the children fall asleep.
11 In that nether, like time’s warm hand upon a woman’s womb 
12 that plants an unspoken thought within a space empty,
13 light shines into darkness turning nothingness into possibility.
